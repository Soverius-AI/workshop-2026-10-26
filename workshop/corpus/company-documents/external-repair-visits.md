# External repair visits

This is an original, fictional company procedure for the Soverius Chocolate RAG lab. All roles, contact routes, and approval rules below are invented for the exercise. It is not an operational safety procedure for a real factory.

## 1. Start with the incident

When an incident appears to require outside repair expertise, the shift manager asks the maintenance coordinator to assess the need for a visit. The request must identify the incident, affected room and equipment, observed symptoms, and any checks already completed.

The maintenance coordinator selects an appropriate service provider and agrees the proposed work. An equipment manual may help identify the relevant expertise, but it does not authorize a visit, room access, or repair.

The cooling-room incident procedure remains responsible for temperature-related escalation. This document adds the external-visit process; it does not change temperature limits or incident-resolution conditions.

## 2. Contact the responsible role

The lab uses role-based contact routes rather than personal telephone numbers. These routes are fictional records, not connected communication tools.

| Need | First contact | Fictional contact route | If unavailable |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Report the incident and coordinate the current shift | Shift manager | Operations duty desk | Plant duty manager |
| Arrange a repair provider and define the work | Maintenance coordinator | Maintenance duty queue | Plant duty manager assigns a qualified maintenance delegate |
| Arrange arrival, check-in, and a temporary badge | Reception/security | Visitor desk | Plant duty manager arranges an authorized check-in officer |
| Decide how products and production areas must be protected | Quality representative | Quality duty queue | Plant duty manager contacts the quality backup |
| Resolve a missing owner or staffing problem | Plant duty manager | Plant duty desk | Visit remains pending until an authorized owner is available |

The current duty roster supplies the person assigned to each role and their current contact details. If that roster is not available, report the missing information. Do not invent a name, phone number, or claim that someone has been contacted.

The plant duty manager may coordinate backups but cannot replace a required maintenance or quality approval merely by being the most senior person present.

## 3. Create a repair-visit record

Before confirming the appointment, the maintenance coordinator creates a visit record linked to the incident. Record:

- the service provider, expected technicians, and relevant competence confirmation;
- the equipment identifier, room, and agreed scope of work;
- expected arrival time, expected duration, and a named internal host;
- tools, replacement parts, and materials expected to enter the site;
- any proposed shutdown, access restriction, or disruption to production;
- the maintenance approver and the quality representative where required.

The host checks that the technician can reach the work area through an agreed route. For work inside either production room, quality reviews how exposed product, packaging, and adjacent activities will be protected before work is authorized.

An urgent or after-hours incident uses the same approval steps. If the required host or approver is unavailable, the technician's visit remains pending; urgency does not create an exception.

## 4. Check in and enter the agreed room

Technicians report to the visitor desk and quote the repair-visit reference. Reception/security verifies their identity against the expected visitor record, confirms the host is available, and records arrival. An unexpected substitute must be confirmed by the maintenance coordinator before access is issued.

The host provides the site briefing, including the agreed route, emergency arrangements, local hygiene requirements, and permitted areas. Any required protective clothing and hygiene steps must be completed before entering a production room.

The temporary badge identifies the approved visit and access period. It does not authorize independent movement around the factory. External technicians remain escorted by the host or an explicitly assigned replacement. If the escort leaves without a handover, technicians return to the designated waiting area.

Room-specific rules for this fictional site:

- **Cooling room:** arrange access without propping open the connecting door. Keep tools and parts away from stored batches and preserve access to walkways. Door position is confirmed by a person; the application has no door sensor.
- **Packaging hall:** use the agreed access route. Keep repair materials away from exposed product and packaging. Entry for observation does not authorize work on the packaging line.

Access to one room does not imply access to the other. A changed work location requires an updated visit record and approval for that area.

## 5. Obtain permission to start the repair

Arrival, briefing, and room access allow the technician to reach the agreed location. They do not authorize repair work to begin.

At the equipment, the technician and maintenance approver confirm the scope and the required controls. Where applicable, authorized site personnel establish and verify equipment isolation under the site's separate isolation procedure. The technician must not infer that equipment is isolated because it appears idle or because the monitoring dashboard shows no activity.

The maintenance approver records permission to start. For work in the Cooling room or Packaging hall, the quality representative must also record that the agreed product and area protections are ready. Any separately required work permit must be in place.

If the required procedure, permit, or approval is missing, the visit stays at **waiting for work authorization**. The assistant should identify the missing item and responsible role.

If diagnosis reveals work outside the agreed scope, pause the affected work and ask the maintenance coordinator to update the plan. Permission to inspect a cooling unit does not automatically authorize electrical modifications or work on nearby equipment.

## 6. Hand back the area and close the visit

The technician records the work performed, parts replaced, remaining issues, and any restrictions. The host checks that tools, spare parts, waste, and temporary barriers have been accounted for and the area has been left ready for review.

Maintenance confirms technical readiness. Quality confirms the production area's readiness and any product restrictions. The shift manager authorizes operational restart only after the necessary maintenance and quality releases are recorded.

Reception/security records departure and closes the temporary access. A technician leaving the site does not mean the equipment is ready to restart. Similarly, closing a repair visit does not resolve the original incident: recovery must still be established under the applicable incident procedure.

## 7. What counts as evidence

| Visit state | Required record |
| --- | --- |
| Requested | Incident link and request for external support |
| Scheduled | Agreed scope, expected technicians, host, and appointment |
| Checked in | Verified arrival and visitor registration |
| Ready for room entry | Completed briefing, area requirements, and escort assignment |
| Authorized to work | Required maintenance and quality approvals, plus applicable controls and permits |
| Handed back | Repair report and the recorded readiness decisions |
| Visit closed | Departure and closure of temporary access |

These records are distinct. A statement such as “I will call maintenance” records an intention, not completed contact. An assistant-generated checklist is guidance, not proof that its steps have been performed. In the lab, human confirmations are explicitly marked as simulated.

## 8. Information that must come from other records

This procedure does not contain current duty assignments, actual contact details, an approved contractor list, room access maps, detailed hygiene instructions, equipment-isolation instructions, or completed visit approvals. Retrieve the relevant current record when available. If it has not been supplied to the lab, identify it as missing rather than filling the gap with an assumption.
